# New English-language quiz categories

Produced 2026-09-16. The agent sampled 28 live prompts to lock the register, then
queried every question she answers below 55% — that query drives the whole list.

**Status key:** ✅ done · 🔍 decide later · ⬜ not started

---

## The measured gaps

She is not weak at "hard" things. She is weak at four specific things:

- **Space and astronomy — her worst domain by far.** Earth closest to the Sun in
  January 33%, stars twinkle 36%, Earth not a sphere 42%, Saturn not the only
  ringed planet 43%, shooting star 43%, Jupiter's mass 44%, Mars' red dust 46%,
  space is silent 54%. The whole "Space and the Sky" subject sits at **78.8%**
  against her 96% average.
- **Everyday and kitchen science.** *White chocolate contains no cocoa solids* —
  **1 correct out of 15 (7%)**, the worst question in the entire bank. She has
  actively learned it wrong. Searing seals juices 29%. Pure water conducts 50%.
- **US-specific facts learned as an adult.** Show-Me State 47% (17 tries),
  capital of Illinois 53% (19 tries), Marbury v. Madison 25%.
- **Ideas rather than events.** Hobbes / *Leviathan* — **0 out of 5**, the only
  zero in the bank.

## ⬜ Top 5 recommendations

1. **🌙 The Night Sky** — *Stars, planets and space, in plain words.*
   The only category where eight measured failures say "she does not know this."
   500+ questions available. Expect 75–85% — the first category below 90%.
   Samples: *Which planet is the hottest?* → **Venus** / Mercury, Mars, Jupiter ·
   *How long does sunlight take to reach Earth?* → **About 8 minutes** / 8 seconds,
   8 hours, No time at all.

2. **🍳 In the Kitchen** — *Food, cooking, and why it works.*
   Cheapest to write, fastest to read, owns the worst question in the bank.
   Misconception density as high as astronomy. 450+ available, 80–88% expected.
   Samples: *What makes bread rise?* → **Gas made by yeast** / Heat from the oven,
   Salt, Kneading · *Which part of a chilli is hottest?* → **The white inner ribs**
   / The seeds, The skin, The stem.

3. **🫀 Your Body** — *How the human body works.*
   The concrete counterpart to a Psychology category that isn't landing. Same
   appetite, fewer abstractions, sustains 600 questions. 85–90%.
   Samples: *Which organ makes insulin?* → **The pancreas** / The liver, The kidney,
   The spleen.

4. **🦊 Animal Lives** — *How animals live and what they can do.*
   The endurance pick — bottomless, reads at her 11-second tempo, plausibly capable
   of a 400-question unbroken run. Cap "biggest/fastest" records at a third or it
   becomes list-trivia. 88–93%.

5. **🗽 Becoming American** — *The country she moved to.*
   The only category with a personal reason to exist, and the data backs the gap.
   The 100 USCIS civics questions are **public domain** and make a clean spine;
   400+ with states, symbols and presidents. Lead with symbols and stories, keep
   constitutional mechanics a minority share.

**Sixth choice if there's room: 📖 Where Words Come From** (etymology). It pays her
back every day in a language she is still acquiring — and one of the answers is
literally *coach*, from Hungarian **kocsi**, the village of Kocs.

## Rejected, with reasons

- **Sports.** Zero engagement signal in 1,300 rounds, proper-noun soup with no
  reasoning path, and culturally gated toward Anglo-American sport. This is the
  one that produces a 40% score that feels like failure rather than discovery.
- **Fashion and Style.** Structurally impossible — no image support, and a
  clothing quiz as four text buttons is a vocabulary test in her second language.
- **Current Events.** Contradicts the architecture. Every question decays, needs a
  migration every few weeks forever, and she'd exhaust a year of news in a fortnight.
- **A generic "Science" category.** It's a bucket, not a subject. The four
  interesting parts of it are Night Sky, Your Body, In the Kitchen and Animal
  Lives — better as four identities than one.
